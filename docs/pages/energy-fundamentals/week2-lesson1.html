Week 2: Energy Sources & Storage

This week, we dive into the origins of the energy that powers our world. Where does it come from? How do we store it for when we need it? You’ll explore the major sources—fossil fuels, renewables, and nuclear—and see how each is harnessed, stored, and delivered. By the end, you’ll understand the tradeoffs that shape our energy system, and why storage is the key to a sustainable future.

Interactive: Explore Energy Sources

Coal
Coal: Abundant, reliable, but high in carbon emissions. Used for baseload power.
Natural Gas
Natural Gas: Flexible, lower emissions than coal, but still a fossil fuel. Used for peaking and backup.
Nuclear
Nuclear: Zero carbon, reliable, but expensive and controversial. Used for baseload power.
Solar
Solar: Clean, renewable, but intermittent. Best for daytime and distributed generation.
Wind
Wind: Clean, renewable, but variable. Best in windy regions and for utility-scale power.
Hydro
Hydro: Clean, reliable, but limited by geography. Used for both baseload and peaking power.

Energy storage is the unsung hero of the modern grid. It allows us to save energy when it’s plentiful and use it when it’s needed most. Without storage, renewables like solar and wind would be far less useful. Let’s explore the main types of energy storage and how they work.

Interactive: Storage Technology Explorer

Battery
Battery: Stores electricity chemically. Fast response, scalable, but limited duration and expensive.
Pumped Hydro
Pumped Hydro: Uses water reservoirs at different heights. Large scale, long duration, but site-limited.
Thermal
Thermal: Stores energy as heat or cold. Useful for buildings and industrial processes.
Flywheel
Flywheel: Stores energy as rotational kinetic energy. Fast response, high cycle life, but limited capacity.
