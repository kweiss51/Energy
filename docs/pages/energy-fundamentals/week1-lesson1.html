Week 1: The Foundations of Energy

Welcome to your first lesson in Energy Fundamentals. This week, we're building the core concepts that all energy professionals must know. Think of this as your physics boot camp. We'll start with the most basic question of all: what is energy? We’ll then explore the two unbreakable laws that govern how energy behaves everywhere in the universe, from a star to a smartphone. By the end of this lesson, you’ll have a new vocabulary and a new way of seeing the world. Everything you see, from a car on the road to a light in a room, is simply a manifestation of these fundamental principles.

Now that we understand the different forms of energy, let's explore its most important rules. First, a concept that is so important it's called a law: the Law of Conservation of Energy, also known as the First Law of Thermodynamics. This law states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only change from one form to another. Every bit of energy you use has been converted from something else. The electrical energy in your home was once chemical energy in a power plant, which was once solar energy stored in a plant millions of years ago.

This brings us to our second key rule: the Second Law of Thermodynamics. While energy is always conserved, it's not always useful. This law explains that when energy is converted, some of it is always lost as unusable thermal energy—or heat. That's why no energy conversion process is ever 100% efficient. For example, a car engine converts chemical energy into kinetic energy, but a lot of that energy is lost as heat from the exhaust. This is a fundamental concept for understanding efficiency and why some energy sources are better than others.
